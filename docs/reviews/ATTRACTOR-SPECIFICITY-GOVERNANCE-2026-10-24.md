# ATTRACTOR Specificity Governance Inquiry — 24 October 2026

**Status:** BOUNDED GOVERNANCE FINDING  
**Scope:** Specificity only  
**Taxonomy review status:** unchanged — registered FAIL remains in force  
**Corpus classification:** not performed  
**Reader-interface work:** out of scope

## Question

What degree of specificity must a prospective evidential route have to perform the institutional function of an ATTRACTOR while preserving State Warrant as the authority for reassessment?

This inquiry does not ask which rule best fits the existing ATTRACTOR corpus. Existing ATTRACTOR texts were not used to choose between the institutional models below.

## 1. Architectural requirements

Existing FCIF implementation and governance material establish the following constraints independently of the taxonomy experiment:

1. **State Warrant retains assessment authority.** The record renderer explicitly states that the complete record and State Warrant retain authority.
2. **Mechanisms are not assessments.** The content-restoration review states that the State Warrant is the authoritative current assessment while mechanisms, lineage and questions document the causal and historical account.
3. **Prospective structures may remain judgment-dependent.** Existing mutation policy explicitly retains editorial/governed judgment where mechanical significance evaluation is not implemented; FCIF does not generally require every prospective relevance test to be reducible to an automatic rule.
4. **ATTRACTOR cannot be assumed to be a complete resolution criterion.** The content-restoration review independently records that several attractors provide enablers rather than complete resolution criteria.
5. **Current guidance requires relationship to the latest assessment to remain explicit.** A mechanism cannot silently supersede or replace the current assessment.

These requirements rule out giving ATTRACTOR independent reassessment authority.

## 2. Institutional models

### Model A — adjudication criterion

An ATTRACTOR must be sufficiently specified that future evidence can be judged as satisfying/not satisfying the ATTRACTOR largely from the object itself.

**Advantages**
- Strong ex-ante determinacy.
- Lower discretion in deciding whether a future event satisfies the object.

**Conflicts with FCIF architecture**
- Tends to turn a mechanism into a shadow decision rule.
- Encourages thresholds to be treated as sufficient conditions for assessment movement even though State Warrant retains authority.
- Is stronger than the existing independent governance statement that some attractors are enablers rather than complete resolution criteria.
- Risks false precision for future evidence whose relevance can be bounded before its eventual evidential weight can be known.

**Finding:** Model A is not warranted as the general institutional meaning of ATTRACTOR specificity.

### Model B — recognisable evidential route

An ATTRACTOR must be sufficiently specified that a later reviewer can determine whether new evidence materially engages the recorded route, while the strength, interpretation and assessment consequence of that evidence remain matters for governed reassessment.

**Advantages**
- Preserves State Warrant authority.
- Makes prospective relevance inspectable and auditable.
- Does not require premature numerical or decisional thresholds.
- Permits a mechanism to identify an enabling or discriminating evidential route without claiming that completion automatically resolves the proposition.

**Risk**
- If "recognisable" is left undefined, Model B collapses into generic research direction or "more evidence needed."

**Finding:** Model B is institutionally warranted only if bounded by a minimum recognisability test.

## 3. Synthetic boundary test

The following examples are synthetic and are not derived from current Frontier Records.

### S1 — insufficient
> More evidence is needed.

Fails. It identifies no prospective route and cannot distinguish relevant future evidence from evidence generally.

### S2 — insufficient
> Independent replication would strengthen the claim.

Fails as a general ATTRACTOR formulation. It identifies a desirable evidential property but does not specify what result/capability/phenomenon is to be replicated or under what material scope.

### S3 — sufficient for recognisability
> An independent laboratory reproduces phenomenon X using the published preparation method and demonstrates measurement Y under condition Z.

Passes the specificity boundary. A later reviewer can determine whether evidence engages the route: independence, X, preparation method, Y and Z are inspectable dimensions. Whether the result is reliable enough to change the assessment remains a separate governed judgment.

### S4 — more specific than institutionally required
> Three independent laboratories must reproduce X within ±2% under Z before the record moves to RESOLVING / VS-04.

The evidential dimensions are recognisable, but the mandatory count, tolerance and automatic state/stage consequence exceed what ATTRACTOR specificity requires unless independently justified by the underlying scientific claim or another ratified rule. Specificity must not be manufactured by adding arbitrary decisional thresholds.

## 4. Minimum recognisability test

For an ATTRACTOR to be sufficiently specific under Model B, its text must allow a later reviewer, without inventing the route anew, to identify:

1. **Target** — the phenomenon, capability, relationship, result or uncertainty the future evidence must engage.
2. **Evidence form** — the material kind of observation, test, replication, demonstration, comparison or development that would engage the route.
3. **Material scope or boundary** — enough contextual constraint to distinguish qualifying engagement from merely adjacent progress.

The object need **not** specify:
- a universal numeric threshold where FCIF has no independently governed basis for one;
- the number of future studies required unless that number is itself institutionally/scientifically warranted;
- the future Pressure State or Verification Stage;
- whether engaging evidence will ultimately be sufficient to establish, reject or resolve the proposition;
- every methodological detail that a later reassessment will need to evaluate evidential weight.

### Operational question

The specificity test is:

> **Could a later reviewer determine from the ATTRACTOR text whether a new evidence item materially engages the recorded prospective route, without inventing a new target, evidence form or material boundary?**

If **no**, specificity fails.

If **yes**, specificity may pass; this says only that the evidence engages the route. It does not say that the ATTRACTOR has been decisively satisfied, that the proposition is established, or that State/Stage must change.

## 5. Qualitative terms

Terms such as "durable", "reliable", "practically relevant", "diverse", "informative" or "material" do not automatically fail specificity.

They are permissible only when the object supplies enough target/evidence-form/scope context that the term constrains an identifiable evidential route rather than carrying the whole criterion by itself.

Synthetic contrast:

- "A durable result would strengthen the claim." — insufficient.
- "Sustained operation of X over repeated full task cycles under production conditions, with failure mode Y monitored." — potentially recognisable even if "sustained" is not assigned an arbitrary universal duration.

A later assessment may still need judgment about evidential weight. That is not equivalent to inventing the prospective route.

## 6. Adversarial checks

### Future technology with no sensible numeric threshold
A route can pass if target, evidence form and material scope are identifiable. Requiring invented numbers would add false precision and improperly turn the mechanism into an adjudication rule.

### Highly precise but irrelevant milestone
Fails despite precision. Specificity cannot substitute for claim relevance.

### Precise event with automatic assessment instruction
The evidential route may be specific, but the automatic transition instruction exceeds ATTRACTOR authority and must not derive authority merely from specificity.

### Broad research programme
Fails where a later reviewer would still have to decide what concrete future evidence counts as engaging the route.

### Enabling development
Can satisfy specificity if the enabling development itself is the recorded prospective route and is materially bounded. This does not make the enabler a complete resolution criterion.

## 7. Governance finding

**Model B — recognisable evidential route — is the better fit to FCIF's existing authority architecture.**

The decisive distinction is:

> **ATTRACTOR specificity governs recognisability of future evidential engagement, not automatic adjudication of future evidence.**

A sufficiently specific ATTRACTOR identifies a target, evidence form and material boundary well enough that later evidence can be recognised as engaging the route. State Warrant retains authority over evidential weight, reassessment, Pressure State and Verification Stage.

This conclusion is derived from FCIF authority separation and synthetic boundary testing, not from attempting to maximise the number of existing ATTRACTOR objects that pass.

## 8. Consequence for the taxonomy work

This inquiry does **not**:
- alter the registered taxonomy FAIL;
- ratify ATTRACTOR;
- revise the failed pre-registration;
- reclassify any of the 30 objects;
- authorise FR-AM-0001 or FR-AM-0002 repairs;
- authorise reader-facing ATTRACTOR UI;
- authorise a v2 taxonomy review.

It resolves only the institutional Specificity question.

A future decision on whether a taxonomy v2 is worthwhile may now treat specificity as requiring **recognisable evidential engagement**, not a complete future adjudication rule. Any v2 would still require a new pre-registration and two genuinely clean classifiers.
