## Decision

**Admit as IN-006 (supportive); append AS-003; reaffirm FRAGMENTING / VS-03.** This is a normal review of new evidence. It does not revise the LPR-001-D27 marker, its receipt, historical instances or historical assessments.

The study used 2,916 plasma proteins from 44,498 UK Biobank participants to estimate the ages of 11 organs. Baseline organ-age gaps associated with later disease and all-cause mortality over 2–17 years. Especially aged brain status was associated with subsequent Alzheimer's disease (HR 3.11 after adjustment for age, sex and APOE genotype). The mortality analysis retained associations after adjustment for PhenoAge and cystatin C. This directly bears on the record's blood-panel and pre-symptomatic outcome elements, and materially strengthens prospective organ-specific evidence.

## Boundary

These are observational cohort associations, with outcome analysis centered on UK Biobank. The Stanford data in the paper help assess the age model across cohorts and platforms; they do not establish external calibration of disease-specific absolute risk or clinical utility. Hazard ratios and age-prediction accuracy do not by themselves tell an individual what their absolute risk is, whether an assay transports to a different population and platform, or whether score-guided action improves health. The authors also note that a cross-sectional age gap only approximates an individual's aging-rate history. This narrows the remaining question to externally validated patient-level prediction, calibration and appropriate decisions, rather than a purported lack of prospective organ-specific signal in blood.

The review updates BN-001, RM-001 and AT-001 to acknowledge the longitudinal cohort evidence, and adds a 2025 lineage entry. OQ-001 remains live; no commercial-product assertion is inferred. The 2024 Argentieri proteomic clock and other neighboring papers were not adjudicated in this bounded candidate review.
