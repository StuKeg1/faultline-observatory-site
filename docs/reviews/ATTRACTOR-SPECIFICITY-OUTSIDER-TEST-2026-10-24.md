**Restated rule:** A route is specific enough when a later reviewer can tell from its text alone what the future evidence must engage (target), what kind of evidence would count (evidence form), and what context separates qualifying evidence from merely adjacent progress (material boundary). It doesn’t have to give numeric thresholds, the number of studies, future states or the expected outcome. Qualitative words are acceptable only when the target, evidence form and boundary carry the criterion, not the qualitative word on its own.

**S1 — NOT SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** There is no target, no evidence form and no boundary, so any new item could be claimed to engage it.

**S2 — NOT SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The evidence form is given (independent replication). The target is only “the claim”, with no phenomenon or result named, and there is no boundary, so a reviewer couldn’t tell which replication qualifies.

**S3 — SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The target is phenomenon X. The evidence form is independent reproduction using the published method, with measurement Y. The boundary is condition Z and the published preparation method.

**S4 — SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The target is X, the evidence form is independent reproduction, and the boundary is within ±2% under Z. The study count and the RESOLVING / VS-04 transition are things the rule says a route need not specify. Under this rule alone, including them doesn’t make the route fail specificity, though they go beyond what specificity asks.

**Fresh Case A — SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The target is autonomous end-to-end task completion by system X. The evidence form is a field trial with failures and human interventions recorded. The boundary is the specified task under normal operating conditions. “Normal” doesn’t carry the criterion alone, because the task and the field-trial setting constrain it.

**Fresh Case B — NOT SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The target is vague: “practically useful” carries the whole criterion. There is no evidence form, only “further improvements”, and no boundary separating qualifying progress from adjacent progress.

**Fresh Case C — SPECIFIC ENOUGH.** The target is X’s performance relative to the best available conventional method. The evidence form is independent comparative testing that reports performance, failure rate and resource use. The boundary is the stated workload under the same input conditions.
